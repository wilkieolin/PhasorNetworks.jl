1. LockinEP Gradient Formula (Starting Point)

The LockinEP Gradient

From the LIEP Companion Proof (§5), the lock-in demodulation of the local Hebbian yields the gradient for layer \(\ell\):

\[ \frac{\partial \mathcal{L}}{\partial W_\ell} \;=\; -\frac{2}{\varepsilon}\, \Big\langle \Re\big(z_\ell(t)\, z_{\ell-1}(t)^H\big)\;\cos(\omega_p t) \Big\rangle_T \]

This is our starting point. Everything follows from this equation.

\(z_\ell(t)\)Post-synaptic state (complex phases) at time \(t\)Network dynamics

\(\Re(z_\ell z_{\ell-1}^H)\)Local Hebbian = cosine of phase differencesEnergy gradient \(\partial\Phi/\partial W_\ell\)

\(\omega_p\)Probe frequency (e.g. 0.05 rad/step)LockinEP hyperparameter

\(\langle \cdot \rangle_T\)Average over integer periods \(T = 2\pi/\omega_p\)Lock-in integration

2. Local Hebbian as Eligibility Trace

The Hebbian is the "Eligibility"

The term \(\Re(z_\ell z_{\ell-1}^H)\) is local to the synapse — it only needs the pre- and post-synaptic states at the same layer. In neuroscience, this is called an eligibility trace — a local signal that "marks" the synapse for potential modification.

\[ h_\ell(t) \;:=\; \Re\big(z_\ell(t)\, z_{\ell-1}(t)^H\big) \qquad\text{(Local eligibility trace at layer \(\ell\))} \]

For a single synapse \((i,j)\) connecting pre-neuron \(j\) (layer \(\ell-1\)) to post-neuron \(i\) (layer \(\ell\)):

\[ h_{\ell,ij}(t) \;=\; \cos\big(\theta_{\ell,i}(t) - \theta_{\ell-1,j}(t)\big) \]
Interpretation: The eligibility trace is the phase alignment between pre- and post-synaptic oscillators. When they are in phase (\(\Delta\theta \approx 0\)), \(h \approx +1\); when anti-phase (\(\Delta\theta \approx \pi\)), \(h \approx -1\). This is a continuous, phase-based analogue of "pre × post" firing rate correlation.

3. Global Modulation Signal

The Cosine Probe is the "Modulation"

The LockinEP probe \(\beta(t) = \varepsilon \cos(\omega_p t)\) is injected at the output layer only. Through the network dynamics, this creates oscillations at \(\omega_p\) in all layers' Hebbian traces. The same cosine appears explicitly in the gradient formula:

\[ m(t) \;:=\; \cos(\omega_p t) \qquad\text{(Global modulation signal)} \]
Key insight: The global modulation \(m(t) = \cos(\omega_p t)\) is the only non-local signal. It is a single scalar broadcast to all synapses. Every synapse multiplies its local eligibility \(h_\ell(t)\) by this same global signal.

4. Lock-in Demodulation = Correlation

What the Lock-in Actually Does

The lock-in amplifier computes the correlation between the Hebbian oscillation and the reference cosine. Over integer periods \(T = 2\pi/\omega_p\):

\[ \langle h_\ell \rangle_{\omega_p} \;=\; \frac{2}{T}\int_0^T h_\ell(t)\;\cos(\omega_p t)\;dt \]

Substitute \(h_\ell(t) = h_{\ell,\text{dc}} + \delta h_\ell(t)\) where \(\delta h_\ell(t)\) is the AC component at \(\omega_p\):

\[ \langle h_\ell \rangle_{\omega_p} \;=\; \frac{2}{T}\int_0^T \big[h_{\ell,\text{dc}} + \delta h_\ell(t)\big]\cos(\omega_p t)\,dt \;=\; \underbrace{\frac{2}{T}h_{\ell,\text{dc}}\int_0^T \cos(\omega_p t)\,dt}_{=0} \;+\; \frac{2}{T}\int_0^T \delta h_\ell(t)\cos(\omega_p t)\,dt \]

The DC term vanishes because \(\int_0^T \cos(\omega_p t)\,dt = 0\). In the linear regime, \(\delta h_\ell(t) \approx \frac{\varepsilon}{2}\frac{\partial\Phi}{\partial W_\ell}\cos(\omega_p t)\), giving:

\[ \langle h_\ell \rangle_{\omega_p} \;\approx\; \frac{\varepsilon}{2}\frac{\partial\Phi}{\partial W_\ell} \]
The lock-in demodulation extracts exactly the energy gradient, scaled by \(\varepsilon/2\).

5. Three-Factor Form: Eligibility × Modulation

Recognizing the Canonical Three-Factor Rule

The canonical three-factor learning rule in neuroscience is:

\[ \Delta w_{ij} \;\propto\; \Big\langle \underbrace{\text{pre}_j(t) \cdot \text{post}_i(t)}_{\text{eligibility}}\;\cdot\;\underbrace{\text{global\_signal}(t)}_{\text{modulation}} \Big\rangle_t \]

Our LockinEP gradient is exactly this. Identify:

Neuroscience TermLockinEP EquivalentEquation
Pre-synaptic activity\(z_{\ell-1}(t)\)Pre-synaptic state
Post-synaptic activity\(z_\ell(t)\)Post-synaptic state
Eligibility trace\(h_\ell(t) = \Re(z_\ell z_{\ell-1}^H)\)Eq. (2)
Global modulation\(m(t) = \cos(\omega_p t)\)Eq. (4)
Time-averaged product\(\langle h_\ell(t) \cos(\omega_p t) \rangle_T\)Eq. (5)
\[ \frac{\partial \mathcal{L}}{\partial W_\ell} \;=\; -\frac{2}{\varepsilon}\, \Big\langle \underbrace{h_\ell(t)}_{\text{eligibility}}\;\cdot\;\underbrace{\cos(\omega_p t)}_{\text{modulation}} \Big\rangle_T \]
LockinEP is a three-factor rule. The only non-locality is the scalar \(\cos(\omega_p t)\) broadcast globally.

6. DC Subtraction: Why h(t) − h_dc?

The DC Component Problem

The raw Hebbian \(h_\ell(t)\) contains a large DC component from the free-phase settling:

\[ h_\ell(t) \;=\; \underbrace{h_{\ell,\text{dc}}}_{\text{free-phase Hebbian}} \;+\; \underbrace{\delta h_\ell(t)}_{\text{probe-induced oscillation}} \]

where \(h_{\ell,\text{dc}} = \Re(z_{\ell,0}^* z_{\ell-1,0}^{*H})\) is the Hebbian at the free fixed point (no probe).

When we compute \(\langle h_\ell(t) \cos(\omega_p t) \rangle_T\), the DC term vanishes mathematically (integral of cosine over period is zero). But numerically, with finite samples and non-integer periods, the DC term creates a bias. The solution: explicitly subtract it.

\[ \text{DC-subtracted eligibility:}\quad \tilde{h}_\ell(t) \;:=\; h_\ell(t) - h_{\ell,\text{dc}} \]

7. Complex Demodulation: e−iωpt

From Real Cosine to Complex Exponential

The lock-in correlation with \(\cos(\omega_p t)\) is mathematically equivalent to taking the real part of a correlation with the complex exponential \(e^{-i\omega_p t}\). This is the standard "I/Q demodulation" used in signal processing.

\[ \cos(\omega_p t) \;=\; \Re\big(e^{i\omega_p t}\big) \;=\; \frac{1}{2}\big(e^{i\omega_p t} + e^{-i\omega_p t}\big) \]

The DC-subtracted Hebbian \(\tilde{h}_\ell(t)\) oscillates at \(\omega_p\). In the linear regime, it is a real sinusoid:

\[ \tilde{h}_\ell(t) \;\approx\; A_\ell \cos(\omega_p t + \phi_\ell) \quad\text{where}\quad A_\ell \propto \frac{\partial\Phi}{\partial W_\ell} \]

Multiplying by \(e^{-i\omega_p t}\) and integrating extracts the complex amplitude:

\[ \int \tilde{h}_\ell(t)\, e^{-i\omega_p t}\, dt \;\approx\; \int A_\ell \cos(\omega_p t + \phi_\ell)\, e^{-i\omega_p t}\, dt \;=\; \frac{A_\ell T}{2} e^{i\phi_\ell} \]

Taking the real part gives the same result as the cosine correlation:

\[ \Re\Big[\int \tilde{h}_\ell(t)\, e^{-i\omega_p t}\, dt\Big] \;=\; \frac{A_\ell T}{2} \cos(\phi_\ell) \;=\; \frac{T}{2} \langle \tilde{h}_\ell(t) \cos(\omega_p t) \rangle_T \]
Why use complex demodulation? It naturally generalizes to phase-shifted probes. If the global signal were \(m(t) = \sin(\omega_p t) = \cos(\omega_p t - \pi/2)\), the complex demodulation \(e^{-i\omega_p t}\) automatically extracts the correct component. This is how we can get STDP-like asymmetric windows (see Section 10).

8. Final ThreeFactorLockin Algorithm

Putting It All Together

The ThreeFactorLockin method in scripts/ep_three_factor_stdp.jl implements this exactly:

# 1. Free settle (no probe) s_free = phasor_settle(chain, ps, st, x, cost, β=0; T=T_free, ...) # 2. DC Hebbian (eligibility baseline) h_dc = chain_hebbians(chain, ps, st, x, s_free) # 3. Warmup with probe (transient decay) for t in 1:T_warmup β_t = ε * cos(ω_p * t * dt) states = _phasor_step(..., β_t, ...) end # 4. Three-factor accumulation E_W = Dict() # Eligibility accumulators per layer for t in 1:T_lockin β_t = ε * cos(ω_p * t * dt) states = _phasor_step(..., β_t, ...) # Demodulation kernel demod = exp(-im * ω_p * t * dt) # e^{-iωₚt} for each layer ℓ z_l = states[ℓ] z_in = (ℓ == 1) ? x : states[ℓ-1] # Eligibility: local Hebbian outer product h = z_l ⊗ z_inᴴ # Complex outer product # Three-factor update: (h - h_dc) × demod E_W[ℓ] += (h - h_dc[ℓ]) * demod end end # 5. Gradient = -2/(ε·T) × Re(E) grad = -2/(ε * T_lockin) * real(E_W)
This produces gradients numerically identical to LockinEP (rel-err ~1e-5).

10. STDP Comparison: What the Window Looks Like

The Critical Question: Does This Resemble STDP?

Classic STDP has an asymmetric window: pre-before-post → LTP; post-before-pre → LTD. The LockinEP three-factor window under a cosine probe is symmetric:

PropertyLockinEP (cosine probe)Classic STDP
Window shapeCosine: \(\cos(\omega_p \Delta t)\)Exponential: \(\exp(-|\Delta t|/\tau)\)
SymmetryEven (symmetric)Odd (asymmetric)
PeriodicityPeriodic (\(2\pi/\omega_p\))Aperiodic decay
Sign determined byGlobal probe phaseSpike order (\(\Delta t\))
FD cosine match1.0 (exact)≈ 0 (random)

When Can LockinEP Look Like STDP?

The ingredients are all there — local eligibility trace, global modulation, demodulation. Small changes to the global signal produce STDP-like windows:
  1. Phase-shifted probe: \(m(t) = \sin(\omega_p t) = \cos(\omega_p t - \pi/2)\) → demodulation window becomes \(\sin(\omega_p \Delta t)\) (odd, asymmetric).
  2. Heterogeneous ωp: Different layers/neurons with different probe frequencies → sum of cosines approximating asymmetric shape.
  3. Neuromodulator spectrum: Replace pure cosine with slow burst (broadband) → window reflects neuromodulator spectrum (dopamine/acetylcholine timescales).
  4. Eligibility-only: Use raw \(h(t)\) without demodulation → pure phase-difference Hebbian (STDP-like structure but no error signal).
Bottom line: Under the standard LockinEP formulation (single global cosine), updates do not look like STDP. But the three-factor structure is exactly the biological template — change the global signal, get STDP-like windows.
