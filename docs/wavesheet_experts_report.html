Sparse VSA experts on the PhasorWaveSheet

A transformer-style extension: gated, patch-local expert modules that read, route, and re-bind information into a passing wave — trained on the discrete SSM.

This is the extension to the companion report The PhasorWaveSheet: derivation & implementation, which derives the wave substrate itself — its dispersion, criticality, and discrete-SSM training. Here we build on top of that substrate. Section references of the form "main report §N" point back to it. Same audience: fluent in HDC/VSA and modern ML (state-space models, attention, mixture-of-experts).

1. What the substrate gives us

The main report establishes the pieces this extension depends on. The PhasorWaveSheet is a translation-invariant recurrent lattice of resonate-and-fire neurons that, in the Fourier basis, decouples into \(H\times W\) independent scalar state-space recurrences \(\hat z_q[n{+}1]=M(q)\,\hat z_q[n]+\hat u_q[n]\) with per-mode multiplier \(M(q)=A+g\hat W(q)\) (main report §3–4). It trains entirely on the discrete SSM — a parallel scan for the linear sheet, a deep-equilibrium fixed point for the spike sheet — with no ODE adjoint (main report §5–6). Information rides the shared carrier as phase, and binding a transformation onto a signal is a unit-phasor multiply (phase addition). Those two facts — a selective diagonal SSM and phase-domain binding — are exactly what the expert module below is built from.

2. Sparse experts: read → gate → re-bind

The sheet propagates information; the natural next step is to make it transform information as waves pass through — sparsely and selectively. The design is a bank of experts, each owning a patch of the sheet, that (1) read their patch, (2) gate themselves on or off, and (3) re-bind their output into the wave. This reconciles three literatures inside one substrate.

Biological motivation (and an honest caveat)

The columnar organisation of neocortex [Mountcastle 1997] — a large folded sheet of repeated, locally-computing columns — motivates a repeated, patch-local "expert" unit. The stronger Thousand-Brains claims (every column carries a grid-cell reference frame; a single column learns a complete object model [Hawkins et al. 2019]) are attractive but remain hypothesis, not established fact — an adversarial fact-check of these two specific claims did not sustain them. We therefore ground the repeated-expert unit on Mountcastle columnar repetition and travelling-wave phenomenology only, and treat the reference-frame story as inspiration, not evidence.

2.1 The bind is a diagonal SSM insert

An expert's re-bind \(z_P \mapsto z_P \odot (g\,\varphi_e)\) is a per-site diagonal multiply \(D_e=\mathrm{diag}(g\,\varphi_e)\) — the same kind of object as the SSM transition \(M\). An expert step is thus an input-/state-dependent diagonal transition spliced into the recurrence: the module never leaves the SSM algebra, it merely makes the transition selective, exactly the generalisation Mamba [Gu & Dao 2023] makes to diagonal SSMs. Two subtleties matter for the derivation. First, because a diagonal bind commutes with the diagonal self-propagator \(A\), the sub-cycle placement of the bind is a gauge the per-cycle step absorbs exactly: \(e^{k(T-t_b)}\,D_e\,e^{kt_b}=D_e\,e^{kT}=D_e A\). Second, a spatially local bind is diagonal in the site basis while transport \(M(q)\) is diagonal in the Fourier basis — they do not co-diagonalise, so a bind spliced into the state transition is not a scalar scan; the scan stays scalar only when the selectivity modulates the input injection (or is spatially global).

2.2 Routing: sparsely-gated mixture-of-experts

The gate is a top-1 router in the mould of sparsely-gated MoE [Shazeer et al. 2017], with two ingredients from recent practice. Fine-grained expert segmentation [DeepSeekMoE 2024] gives combinatorial routing flexibility and drives experts toward non-overlapping specialisation. Crucially, load balancing uses the auxiliary-loss-free scheme [DeepSeek 2024]: a per-expert bias is added to the routing scores before the top-\(k\) selection and nudged by each expert's recent load, so utilisation is balanced without injecting the interference gradients that an auxiliary loss would. The discrete choice is made differentiable by a straight-through / Gumbel-softmax estimator [Jang et al. 2016] — hard one-hot in the forward pass, softmax gradient in the backward pass. This is moe_gate / update_moe_bias in the implementation.

2.3 Two routing regimes

A selective SSM stays parallel-scan-trainable iff its coefficients can be computed before the scan.

  • Input-conditioned (regime 1): the gate/bind read the input drive, so the modulated drive is precomputed and the whole augmented sheet is one parallel scan (or spike DEQ). Implemented as a drive modulation \(\text{drive}\odot(1+\sum_e \text{mask}_e\,g_e(\varphi_e-1))\).
  • State-conditioned (regime 2): the gate reads the live wave — the literal "modify a wave as it passes by." Coefficient↔state feedback breaks the global scan, so we use event-based operator splitting: run the linear sheet as a parallel scan within chunks and apply the read→gate→bind event at chunk boundaries. With an identity event this equals the full scan exactly (linear transport composes across boundaries), so the sequential cost is only the number of events — which is small precisely because the experts are sparse.

2.4 Recovering the bind: VSA unbinding and resonators

Because the stamp is a VSA bind, it is recoverable by unbinding — the same algebra that VSA uses to attach and detach transformations from a carrier [Plate 1995]. When several codes are superposed, a resonator network [Frady et al. 2020; Kent et al. 2020] factors the product by searching in superposition; resonators have been realised as multi-compartment spiking phasor neurons on neuromorphic hardware [Renner et al. 2024] — the same substrate as the sheet's spike mode — and FHRR has been merged with deep residual/attention networks [repo author 2022]. Related reconciliations of oscillatory computation with attention/gating include Kuramoto oscillatory neurons composed with attentive connectivity [AKOrN 2024], attention on cellular automata [ViTCA 2022], and travelling-wave RNNs that store and transform sequences [Keller & Welling 2023; Keller et al. 2024]. The toroidal re-entry of the wave has a 1-D analogue in recurrent-depth / looped transformers [Geiping et al. 2025].

3. Empirical results

3.1 The go/no-go: does discrete routing survive the wave substrate?

The central risk of the expert design is that a discrete straight-through gate, reading from and writing into the wave, collapses to one always-on expert. On a synthetic "which patch is coherent" routing task the router reaches 100% accuracy with per-expert load staying spread (load-entropy \(\approx 1.2\) of \(\ln 4 = 1.386\), all four experts used) — no collapse [demos/wave_experts_gonogo.jl]. The straight-through gate gradient stays informative through the discrete-SSM rollout.

3.2 Decode check: is the stamped bind recoverable?

The stamped bind is recoverable: unbinding the output against a bind-free reference recovers every expert's code, robustly and even under an incoherent carrier. A blind decode is coherence-limited (100% → 0% as the carrier decoheres) — the intrinsic square-law/coherence nature of the readable channel. The prototype's per-expert bind is a 1-D scalar phase, so codes on disjoint patches decode independently but cannot be superposed and factored at one site; genuine superposition capacity would require high-D per-site codes plus a resonator.

4. Open questions

  • Superposition capacity. The prototype's scalar-per-expert bind has none; high-D per-site codes + a resonator are the path to carrying multiple bound transforms per wave, and the ceiling vs. hypervector dimension is unmeasured.
  • Scaling. The go/no-go and FashionMNIST runs are small and single-seed; scaling the expert bank, running on the spike-DEQ substrate, and larger real tasks remain to test.
  • State-conditioned routing at depth. The event-split keeps the state-conditioned path parallel within chunks; how gate quality and stability behave as chunks shrink (finer state feedback) is open.
  • Load signal on a torus. The DeepSeek per-expert bias balances token-style load; the right signal for spatially-localised patch occupancy on a 2-D torus is not yet characterised.
