PhasorNetworks — scaling & mechanism findings

What actually scales in the phasor SSM-MLP, aimed at scaling up an audio keyword-spotting network. Companion docs: architecture_report.html (per-test diagrams), lsa_lca_mechanism.html (LSA/LCA mechanisms), RESULTS_TABLE.md, and the FINDINGS*.md files.

TL;DR

  • The architecture is an LRU/S5-style SSM-MLP, not a Transformer. Attention (LSA/LCA) is pointwise in L; all cross-timestep transport is the λ-conv SSM memory.
  • Depth is a weak lever (peaks ~3, regresses at 4). Width and the FFN are load-bearing.
  • Biggest long-range win: SSM state expansion — ffn_n_modes=2 (2 timescales/channel) → 0.73→0.94.
  • λ-range (tau_max) must match the evidence timescale. Head count is a free positive knob (esp. LCA).
  • Head-slicing is not a bottleneck — full-D heads don't help; width does (negative result, confirmed two ways).
  • Audio config: shallow (2–3 blocks), D≈96, FFN n_modes=2, tau_max matched to utterance length, more (LCA) heads.
  • ⚠ Reconciliation (see §Reconciliation): the audio task's no-FFN win exposed that several results here were measured with a single-position readout that over-states temporal-integration knobs. Under the audio-representative pooling readout, "FFN load-bearing" becomes redundant, the modes/λ gains shrink ~3–4×, and the anchors gain vanishes. Width remains the robust lever. The port itself is forward+backward parity-faithful (~1e-6).

The architecture & the probe task

Each block = residual attention (LSA head↔head, or LCA per-head Hopfield retrieval + bind — both pointwise in L) then a residual FFN whose PhasorDense layers hold the causal λ-conv SSM memory. All tests use TIR (Temporal Integration Recall): a target value planted noisily at m spread-out timesteps, one-off random distractors elsewhere, a constant cue at the read position; headless similarity readout (no learnable head → measures the stack, not a head). Chance = 0.0625. Shrunk scale (D=48 base, L=32, 2 seeds) for local tractability.

Knob 2 — Selectivity, and why TIR is blind to it

What "selectivity" means here

Our SSM is Linear Time-Invariant (LTI): the per-channel decay λ and the mode mix-down are fixed after training, so every timestep is integrated with the same dynamics regardless of content. Mamba's central advance is making the SSM parameters input-dependent (selective) — the model decides, per position and from the content, how much to integrate, which timescale to use, or whether to reset memory. That is what lets a selective SSM ignore irrelevant tokens and lock onto relevant ones.

The tractable form for us (builds directly on our biggest win, the 2-mode bank): a per-position, content-dependent gate g(x) ∈ (0,1) applied to the SSM input before the (still-LTI) causal convolution, so distractor/irrelevant frames are down-weighted before they ever enter memory. (Full input-dependent λ would require a sequential scan, breaking the cheap conv; the input-gate keeps the conv LTI.)

Why our TIR task cannot reveal its benefit

Selectivity only pays off when per-position content signals the right behavior. TIR violates this in two ways:

  • Fixed readout, fixed optimal dynamics. TIR reads out at a single fixed position (the cue at L), integrating a decaying superposition of all prior frames. The best timescale / amount to integrate is determined by the task structure, fixed per example — a content gate has nothing example-specific to switch on, so it would learn a constant and collapse to the LTI baseline.
  • Relevance isn't pointwise-decidable. TIR distractors are one-off random values; whether a frame is signal or distractor is only knowable from cross-frame consistency (the value that recurs) — which no pointwise gate can see. So even an input gate has no per-frame signal to act on.

Consequence: a selectivity experiment on TIR would return a null result by construction — selective and non-selective converge to the same behavior. That would be uninformative, not evidence that selectivity is useless.

What task would reveal it

One where per-position content marks relevance in a pointwise-decidable way. Concretely: relevant frames carry the target value tagged with a detectable marker (e.g. in dedicated marker channels); distractor frames carry other valid values with a different/absent marker. Then:

  • A non-selective (LTI) model integrates everything → distractors dilute the signal → accuracy falls as distractor count grows.
  • A selective model learns g≈1 on marked frames, g≈0 elsewhere → integrates only the relevant value → stays accurate.

The gap between them is the selectivity benefit. This is the phasor analog of Mamba's selective-copying / filtering benchmarks. Testing knob 2 therefore requires building this task alongside the input-gate mechanism — it is a two-part investigation, not a drop-in sweep.

Reconciliation with the audio task & corrected regime

The audio 30-class keyword-spotting HPO (phasor_torch on Aurora) found the opposite of the FFN result here: its best net (79.3%) uses no FFN. Chasing this down (the "linchpin": the same FFN on/off ablation run in both frameworks) revealed that several results above were readout artifacts. TIR used a single-position headless readout; the audio pipeline has an input embedding + a pooling readout (SSMReadout over the last 25%) that already does the temporal integration the FFN/modes/λ knobs provide. Adding both to Julia TIR flips the FFN from load-bearing (Δ+0.18) to redundant (Δ+0.02), matching audio.

The port is faithful: a new backward-parity harness shows Julia Zygote ≈ torch autograd to ~1e-6 for PhasorDense and PhasorLCA (incl. the near-origin gate); with pipeline + optimizer matched the two frameworks agree end-to-end (0.435 vs 0.448). The optimizer ρ (Julia RMSProp 0.9 vs torch 0.99) is a minor level shift. See LINCHPIN_FINDINGS.md.

  • LogSumExp-over-time pooling (R4) — biggest structural lever: lifts accuracy further (attn-only 0.448→0.543), flips the FFN delta negative (attn-only now beats the FFN model), and collapses the modes advantage (Δ→+0.005). A max-over-time readout does the temporal aggregation itself → the FFN and the mode-bank become redundant-to-harmful. Strongest reproduction of audio's "no-FFN wins," and it explains the mechanism (KWS = "is the keyword present somewhere").
  • Learnable codes / β (R2, R3) — negligible. The loss and pooling are the levers, not prototype geometry.
  • Total Tier-1 gain: +0.10 (FFN) to +0.16 (attn-only) — entirely in the readout/loss.

Audio action: swap similarity_loss → softmax-CE and SSMReadout mean-pool → logsumexp-over-time on the real net; expect a bump past 79.3%, largest for the no-FFN config.

Recommendations for the audio scale-up

  • Readout first (biggest lever): contrastive softmax-CE + logsumexp-over-time pooling — the largest accuracy gain (+0.10–0.16), and it makes the FFN/modes redundant.
  • Scale by widening, not deepening: shallow (2–3 blocks), D≈96 (the robust lever, holds under the corrected regime).
  • The FFN is optional if you have a pooling readout (as audio does) — it's redundant then; audio's no-FFN win is real. If you drop the pooling/frontend, restore the FFN. A 2-mode FFN (ffn_n_modes=2) still gives a modest long-range edge.
  • Match tau_max to the utterance/evidence timescale (long inputs → large τ; dense/short → moderate) — still holds, effect smaller with pooling.
  • Anchors: not a reliable knob under the pooling readout (was a single-position artifact) — don't over-invest.
  • Favor LCA (its learned-anchor retrieval + binding drives the ~16 pt LCA≫LSA gap on audio).
  • Then pursue selectivity (knob 2) with a relevance-markable task — the natural frontier once the static levers are set.
